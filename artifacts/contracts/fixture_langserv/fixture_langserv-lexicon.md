## First-read anchors

A reader who reads nothing else reads these seven.

1. **Keep subject measurement separate from instrument assurance.** The fixture server is the measuring instrument. Serena and SolidLSP are the subject under test. Subject adjudications measure the subject; self-checks assure the instrument and can never produce a subject finding. The instrument belongs to no subject. (B-1, T-lsf, T-self-check)
2. **Expectations flow one way.** The expectation record is read by the assertion suite and by no other component, ever. If the fixture server can read it, every test is vacuous. (B-2)
3. **Scenario-specified behaviour is conforming instrument behaviour.** When the fixture server withholds a response or aborts under a pinned fault scenario, the instrument is working as specified even though the resulting exchange intentionally violates a protocol obligation. Protocol-permitted delay, notification traffic, and response reordering are perturbations, not injected faults. (B-3)
4. **Never write a bare position.** Every position, range, and offset carries its unit and origin. LanguageServerFixture's ratified instrument invariant sets the negotiated LSP wire encoding to UTF-16, with zero-based line and character values. (B-4)
5. **Four layers, four words.** fixture declaration → symbol emission → normalized symbol → tool result. One word for two layers is a defect in the built system. (B-6)
6. **The fixture language is never a supported Serena language.** Test-only wiring only. Any sentence that registers it as production language support is a violation. (B-7)
7. **A subject finding is the last resort, not the first.** An observed non-conformance is a baseline defect, fixture defect, launch-harness defect, or assertion-suite defect until all four are excluded. (FO-6)

**Closure check.**
- Every act appears exactly once, with exactly one verb and exactly one authority. No verb is shared.
- Every registry entry appears as an authority except AR-2, AR-6, AR-7.
- AR-2 corpus baseline is a truth-holder and deliberately act-free: meaning does not act.

## 3. Standing boundaries

The separations this system depends on. Each is stated once. None is reopenable except by ACT-1.

**B-1 — Measurement boundary (instrument side / subject side).**
The **instrument side** is the fixture server, the corpus baseline, the launch harness, and the assertion suite. The **subject side** is Serena and SolidLSP. In a subject adjudication, the instrument establishes a known quantity and the subject is measured against it. A **self-check** is separate instrument assurance: it may measure fixture-server conformance to the corpus baseline and pinned scenario, but it never produces a subject finding. Vocabulary remains disjoint: instrument assurance is never narrated as subject measurement, and nothing on the subject side is ever described as expected, correct, or authoritative.

**B-2 — Expectation direction.**
The expectation record flows to the assertion suite and to nothing else. The fixture server never reads it, never imports it, never derives from it, and never shares a module with it. A shared derivation makes the instrument and the assertion agree by construction, and the test measures nothing. This boundary has no exception and no override.

**B-3 — Scenario-behaviour boundary (protocol-permitted perturbation / injected fault / defect).**
A **protocol-permitted perturbation** is non-baseline behaviour that LSP or JSON-RPC permits, such as a stated delay, stated notification traffic, response reordering that preserves correctness, or a stated permitted error response in place of a baseline result. An **injected fault** is scenario-specified behaviour that intentionally leaves a protocol obligation unsatisfied or emits a protocol-impermissible payload, such as withholding, aborting mid-request, or a malformed emission. Both are conforming instrument behaviour under their pinned scenarios. A **defect** is behaviour no scenario asked for. Instrument conformance and protocol conformance are therefore separate: an injected fault can be conforming instrument behaviour while intentionally protocol-non-conforming.

**B-4 — Position-unit boundary.**
LSP 3.17 permits UTF-8, UTF-16, and UTF-32 position encodings; UTF-16 is the fallback when the server does not select another advertised client encoding. LanguageServerFixture deliberately sets the negotiated wire encoding to UTF-16 as a ratified instrument invariant. A **wire position** in this system is therefore a zero-based line and zero-based UTF-16 code-unit character offset. Every other offset — byte, Unicode scalar, grapheme, column — is a distinct kind and carries its unit in the noun. The fixture corpus contains non-ASCII identifiers precisely so that a collapsed unit produces a visible off-by-n rather than a silent conforming outcome.

**B-5 — Lifecycle boundary (protocol lifecycle / process lifecycle / resource collection).**
The conforming protocol shutdown sequence contains three messages: the shutdown request, the fixture server's shutdown response, and the exit notification. Process termination is an operating-system event; reaping is subsequent collection of the exited child. These are separate. Retire, abort, external termination, and reap are four acts and never one word.

**B-6 — Layer boundary.**
Four representations, four terms, disjoint: **fixture declaration** (the fixture server's internal model) → **symbol emission** (the object on the wire) → **normalized symbol** (what SolidLSP produces from it) → **tool result** (what a Serena tool returns to its caller). The whole point of the instrument is to detect divergence between adjacent layers; a word that spans two layers makes that divergence unwritable.

**B-7 — Support boundary.**
The fixture language is wired for tests only. It is never registered as Serena language support, never listed among supported languages, never given a production language-server configuration, and never documented as a Serena capability. Crossing this boundary defeats an acceptance criterion of the project.

**B-8 — Design time / run time.**
Developing the instrument versus the instrument in use. Side markers are carried in §1. Exactly two crossings are sanctioned:
- **design → run:** ACT-1 ratify. A ratified corpus baseline becomes the live measurement standard. No other design-side act changes run-time standing.
- **run → design:** recorded evidence. Transcripts, non-conforming outcomes, and divergence detections may initiate the amendment path (§16) and nothing else.
A term spans the boundary only by explicit declaration on the closed spanning list. **The corpus baseline and the failure-outcome taxonomy (FO-1..FO-5) are the spanning set.** An unsided registry entry fails the closure check.

## 4. Failure-outcome taxonomy

The closed set of outcomes for an observed non-conformance. This is the spanning set for B-8.

| ID | Outcome | Meaning | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| FO-1 | **baseline defect** | The expectation record disagrees with the fixture corpus text. The measurement standard is wrong. | Amendment to the corpus baseline; re-ratification by AR-1. |
| FO-2 | **fixture defect** | The fixture server does not conform to the corpus baseline, or does not produce its pinned scenario's behaviour. The instrument is wrong. | Fix the instrument. Never reported as a subject finding. |
| FO-3 | **launch-harness defect** | The launch harness assembled, pinned, terminated, or reaped incorrectly. | Fix the launch harness. |
| FO-4 | **assertion-suite defect** | The assertion suite observed, compared, or adjudicated incorrectly. | Fix the assertion suite. |
| FO-5 | **subject finding** | The subject under test behaved non-conformingly against a correct instrument, correct corpus baseline, correct launch harness, and correct assertion suite. | Reported against Serena or SolidLSP. |

**FO-6 — Exclusion order.** An observed non-conformance is FO-1, FO-2, FO-3, or FO-4 until all four are excluded. Only then is it FO-5. A suite that reports a subject finding without recording the exclusion has produced a claim, not a finding.

## 6. Term admission

Every candidate term passes all five. Failing any one means the term is not admitted.

1. **Dominant-meaning alignment.** Cold, would the median reader's prior land on the intended authority placement? Measured against protocol specifications, Serena's own source and documentation, and bare-term probes — not argued from aptness.
2. **One term per concept.** A reserved term already covering the concept ends the question.
3. **Form distance.** Reject one-morpheme neighbours of reserved terms, and compounds that read as variants of them. Reject collisions with the engine's reserved tokens.
4. **Anti-definition ready at coin time.** If what the term is *not* cannot be stated now, the concept is not crisp enough to name.
5. **Fits the qualification system** (§9), without ad-hoc intensifiers.

**Rejections are recorded** in §10 or in an entry's *Not:* field, with the reason. Unrecorded rejections get re-litigated by every writer who independently rediscovers the obvious word.

**T-reap** — **reap.** The launch harness's act of waiting for an already-exited child process, collecting its exit status, and allowing operating-system resources to be released. *Authority:* AR-4. *Related:* terminate externally, abort, retire, server lifetime. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not an act that ends a running server lifetime. **T-record** — **record.** The fixture server's act of appending one line to the transcript. ### 7C — Protocol surface

**T-protocol-request** — **protocol request.** A framed JSON-RPC message carrying an identifier. At the protocol layer, every processed request is owed exactly one protocol response or protocol error response. Under a pinned fault scenario, the fixture server may intentionally leave that obligation unsatisfied by withholding or aborting; this is conforming instrument behaviour and intentionally protocol-non-conforming (B-3). *Authority:* AR-7 sends; AR-3 answers or injects the specified fault. *Related:* protocol notification, protocol response, withhold, abort. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a notification — notifications carry no identifier and are owed nothing. Not a call — *call* imports call/return and hides identifiers and ordering. *Collision:* failing to distinguish protocol obligation from scenario-specified violation either erases the fault scenario or falsely claims the protocol permits it. *Docs:* "the protocol request." *Prompts:* "a protocol request carries an identifier and is owed exactly one response at the protocol layer; only a pinned fault scenario may intentionally leave that obligation unsatisfied." *Violation:* "Respond to the didOpen call." → write: "textDocument/didOpen is a protocol notification; emit nothing in reply."

**T-protocol-notification** — **protocol notification.** A framed JSON-RPC message carrying no identifier, for which nothing is owed. *Authority:* AR-7 or AR-3 may send. *Related:* protocol request, notification burst. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a request. Not a message the reader may answer — emitting a reply to a notification is a protocol violation and, if the fixture server does it unasked, a fixture defect. Not an alert or user-facing notice. *Collision:* the everyday sense of *notification* carries no protocol obligations, while server-originated notifications are easily misclassified as unsolicited faults. *Docs:* "the protocol notification." *Prompts:* "notifications carry no identifier and receive no reply; server notifications are not faults merely because no request solicited them." *Violation:* "The server acknowledges the initialized notification." → write: "The fixture server records the initialized notification and emits nothing."

**T-protocol-response** — **protocol response.** The single framed message the fixture server emits carrying the result for one identified protocol request. *Authority:* AR-3. *Related:* emit, protocol error response, response reordering. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a return value. Not an error response — an emitted error is a distinct message form. Not necessarily ordered by request arrival — LSP permits response reordering when correctness is unaffected. *Collision:* readers assume responses arrive in request order or misclassify permitted response reordering as a fault. *Docs:* "the protocol response." *Prompts:* "one protocol response per identified request, emitted in the order the pinned scenario specifies; permitted response reordering is not an injected fault." *Violation:* "Responses must come back in the order requests were sent." → write: "The fixture server may reorder responses where LSP permits it; the pinned scenario states the exact order."

**T-protocol-error-response** — **protocol error response.** A framed message carrying a JSON-RPC error object for one identified protocol request. It satisfies the request's response obligation and is protocol-conforming when its code and payload are permitted for that request. *Authority:* AR-3. *Related:* protocol request, malformed emission, error-response substitution. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not automatically an injected fault or defect. Not a malformed emission — an error response is structurally valid. Not an absent response. Not by itself a scenario class — the error-response substitution names the perturbation that emits one in place of a baseline result. *Collision:* the word *error* triggers defect-handling reflexes even though an error response is a normal protocol response form. *Docs:* "the fixture server emits a protocol error response." *Prompts:* "a protocol error response satisfies the request's response obligation; adjudicate its code and payload against the pinned scenario." *Violation:* "The server errored on workspace/symbol." → write: "Under the pinned scenario, the fixture server emitted the stated protocol error response for the workspace/symbol request."

**T-wire-position** — **wire position.** In LanguageServerFixture, a zero-based line and a zero-based UTF-16 code-unit character offset under the instrument's ratified LSP position-encoding invariant (B-4). *Authority:* AR-1 ratifies the invariant; AR-2 fixes corpus values; AR-3 selects or falls back to UTF-16 during initialization and emits positions in that encoding. *Related:* name range, declaration range, fixture corpus. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a claim that LSP permits only UTF-16 — LSP 3.17 also permits UTF-8 and UTF-32. Not a byte or Unicode-scalar offset. Not a column or editor position. *Collision:* implementations default to their host language's indexing model; the error is invisible on ASCII-only input. *Docs:* "the wire position (zero-based line, UTF-16 code unit)." *Prompts:* "this instrument negotiates or falls back to UTF-16; every wire position is a zero-based line and zero-based UTF-16 code-unit offset." *Violation:* "LSP positions are always UTF-16." → write: "LanguageServerFixture sets its negotiated LSP position encoding to UTF-16 as a ratified instrument invariant."

**T-name-range** — **name range.** The wire-position range covering only the declared identifier of a fixture declaration. *Authority:* AR-2. *Related:* declaration range, wire position, fixture declaration. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not the declaration range; not the whole declaration; not an editor selection. *Collision:* LSP distinguishes the enclosing `range` from the more focused `selectionRange`; a loose range word silently merges them. *Docs:* "the name range." *Prompts:* "the name range covers the identifier only; the declaration range encloses the complete declaration. Never one word for both." *Violation:* "The symbol's range is lines 7 to 9." → write: "The declaration range spans lines 7 to 9; the name range covers the identifier on line 7."

**T-declaration-range** — **declaration range.** The wire-position range enclosing the complete fixture declaration, from its opening keyword through its closing delimiter, including its declared name and nested declarations. *Authority:* AR-2. *Related:* name range, fixture declaration. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a body range — *body* normally excludes the declaration header, while this range includes it. Not the name range. Not scope — scoping is a semantic relation. *Collision:* the previous term *body range* contradicted its own extent and invited agents to exclude the header. *Docs:* "the declaration range." *Prompts:* "the declaration range encloses the complete fixture declaration; the name range covers only the declared identifier." *Violation:* "Rename replaced the declaration range." → write: "The rename edit replaced the text at the name range; the declaration range is unchanged."

**T-document-version** — **document version.** The integer the subject under test attaches to a text-document state through the protocol's synchronization messages. It increases after each change, including undo and redo; increments need not be consecutive. *Authority:* AR-7 assigns; AR-3 observes. *Related:* protocol notification. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a release version or corpus baseline amendment. Not permission to send decreasing versions. A future stale-work scenario must concern work or output based on an earlier document version, not a non-monotonic synchronization sequence. *Collision:* *version* fuses protocol document state with software releases and baseline amendments, while *stale version* can incorrectly imply that the synchronization version itself may decrease. *Docs:* "document version N." *Prompts:* "document versions increase after every content change; name the exact integer and describe older work relative to it." *Violation:* "Send an older document version to simulate stale work." → write: "Keep document versions increasing; if required, design a scenario whose work or output is based on an earlier document version."

**T-protocol-shutdown-sequence** — **protocol shutdown sequence.** Three protocol messages in order: the subject under test sends the shutdown request; the fixture server emits the shutdown response; the subject under test sends the exit notification. *Authority:* AR-7 sends the request and notification; AR-3 emits the response. *Related:* retire, terminate externally, reap, B-5. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a pair. Not process termination — the sequence precedes retire and does not by itself establish that the process exited. Not a guarantee that the lifetime ends — a pinned fault scenario may intentionally ignore an obligation. *Collision:* readers omit the shutdown response or equate a sent request with process exit. *Docs:* "the protocol shutdown sequence." *Prompts:* "shutdown request, shutdown response, then exit notification; only after the exit notification may the fixture server retire." *Violation:* "The client sends shutdown and exit." → write: "The subject under test sends the shutdown request, receives the fixture server's shutdown response, then sends the exit notification."

### 7D — Semantic layers (B-6)

**T-fixture-declaration** — **fixture declaration.** A named construct in fixture-language source — module, import, namespace, definition, or theorem — as held in the fixture server's internal model. Layer 1. *Authority:* AR-2 fixes what exists; AR-3 holds the model. *Related:* corpus declaration path, occurrence, symbol emission. *Aliases:* *declaration* bare is permitted inside a paragraph that has already written *fixture declaration*; never bare in prompts. *Not:* not a symbol — *symbol* names three different things across the layers and is banned bare; write *fixture declaration*, *symbol emission*, or *normalized symbol*. **T-symbol-emission** — **symbol emission.** The protocol object the fixture server emits to describe a fixture declaration — hierarchical or flat, per the pinned scenario's stated response-shape parameter. Layer 2. *Authority:* AR-3. *Related:* fixture declaration, normalized symbol, emit. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not the fixture declaration — write *fixture declaration* for the internal model. Not the normalized symbol — write *normalized symbol* for what SolidLSP produces. Not one fixed shape — scenarios state hierarchical or flat response-shape parameters deliberately. *Collision:* one word for all three layers makes normalization divergence — the thing the instrument exists to detect — literally unwritable. *Docs:* "the symbol emission." *Prompts:* "a symbol emission is the wire object; what SolidLSP makes of it is a normalized symbol, and they are compared, never assumed equal." *Violation:* "Serena receives the symbols the server produces." → write: "The subject under test receives symbol emissions and produces normalized symbols from them."

Subject side. *Authority:* AR-7 produces it; it holds no authority. *Related:* symbol emission, tool result, subject finding. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not the symbol emission — write *symbol emission*. Not correct by construction — divergence between emission and normalized symbol is a candidate subject finding and is exactly what is measured. Not the tool result. *Collision:* readers assume normalization is transparent; the whole second milestone exists because it is not. *Docs:* "the normalized symbol." *Prompts:* "normalized symbols are subject-side output; adjudicate them against the corpus baseline, never assume they mirror the emission." *Violation:* "The normalized symbols show the correct ranges." → write: "The normalized symbols carry ranges; the assertion suite adjudicates them against the corpus baseline."

Subject side. *Authority:* AR-7 produces it; it holds no authority. *Related:* normalized symbol, subject finding. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not the normalized symbol — write *normalized symbol* for the SolidLSP layer. Not the protocol response. Not authoritative about the corpus — the corpus baseline is. *Collision:* end-to-end assertions are habitually written against tool results while being described as testing the protocol layer, which loses the ability to localise a defect. *Docs:* "the tool result." *Prompts:* "tool results are the outermost subject-side layer; name the layer every assertion targets." *Violation:* "The end-to-end test checks the LSP response." → write: "The assertion suite adjudicates the tool result; a separate adjudication targets the normalized symbol."

### 7E — Scenarios, perturbations, and injected faults

**T-scenario** — **scenario.** The named, closed specification of fixture-server run-time behaviour for one server lifetime, pinned at launch and immutable thereafter. Exactly one of baseline scenario, perturbation scenario, or fault scenario. Every scenario additionally states its response-shape parameter — hierarchical or flat — governing symbol emissions; response shape is a scenario parameter, never a scenario class. *Authority:* AR-4 pins it; AR-3 executes it. *Related:* pin, baseline scenario, perturbation scenario, fault scenario, invocation ordinal. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a test case or BDD narrative; scenarios contain no assertions. Not a mode that changes within a lifetime. *Collision:* test traditions invite readers to embed assertions and blur protocol-permitted perturbations with injected faults. *Docs:* "the pinned scenario." *Prompts:* "a scenario names fixture-server behaviour only, is classified as baseline, perturbation, or fault, states its response-shape parameter, and contains no assertions." *Violation:* "The hang scenario checks that Serena times out." → write: "The withholding fault scenario specifies that the fixture server withholds the response; the assertion suite adjudicates whether the subject under test bounded its wait."

**T-baseline-scenario** — **baseline scenario.** The scenario under which the fixture server injects neither perturbation nor fault: every processed request is answered with the result the corpus baseline fixes, well-formed, in arrival order, within the configured bound. *Authority:* AR-4 pins it. *Related:* scenario, perturbation scenario, fault scenario. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not the healthy scenario. Not an implicit default — it is pinned explicitly. Not the corpus baseline. *Collision:* the shared morpheme with *corpus baseline* remains a live collision; *nominal scenario* is an unadmitted alternative. *Docs:* "the baseline scenario." *Prompts:* "write baseline scenario in full and keep it distinct from corpus baseline; it is pinned explicitly." *Violation:* "Run the healthy server for the symbol tests." → write: "Launch a server lifetime with the baseline scenario pinned."

**T-perturbation-scenario** — **perturbation scenario.** A non-baseline scenario that specifies exactly one protocol-permitted variation at a stated method and invocation ordinal. The admitted perturbations are an injected delay, an exact notification burst, response reordering that preserves correctness, and an error-response substitution. *Authority:* AR-4 pins it. *Related:* injected delay, notification burst, response reordering, error-response substitution, invocation ordinal. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a fault scenario — it intentionally remains protocol-conforming. Not random, concurrent, or approximate. *Collision:* test prose habitually calls every non-baseline behaviour a fault, hiding which scenarios actually violate protocol obligations. *Docs:* "a perturbation scenario." *Prompts:* "a perturbation scenario specifies one exact, protocol-permitted variation; never call it an injected fault." *Violation:* "Inject an out-of-order fault." → write: "Pin the response-reordering perturbation scenario with the exact response order."

**T-fault-scenario** — **fault scenario.** A non-baseline scenario that specifies exactly one injected fault at a stated method and invocation ordinal. *Authority:* AR-4 pins it. *Related:* injected fault, withhold, abort, malformed emission. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not any non-baseline scenario — protocol-permitted variations belong to perturbation scenarios. Not a broken server or negative test. Not composable: exactly one injected fault per fault scenario. *Collision:* fault-injection vocabulary reads as chaos and randomness; here every injected fault is exactly specified and reproducible. *Docs:* "a fault scenario." *Prompts:* "a fault scenario specifies exactly one intentionally protocol-non-conforming injected fault at a named method and invocation ordinal." *Violation:* "Randomly inject failures to see what breaks." → write: "Pin one fault scenario naming one injected fault, method, and invocation ordinal."

**T-protocol-permitted-perturbation** — **protocol-permitted perturbation.** One exact, non-baseline variation that remains within LSP and JSON-RPC permissions and is specified by a perturbation scenario. *Authority:* AR-3 produces it under the scenario pinned by AR-4. *Related:* perturbation scenario, injected delay, notification burst, response reordering, error-response substitution. *Aliases:* *perturbation* only after the full term appears in the same paragraph; never bare in prompts. *Not:* not an injected fault — it violates no protocol obligation. Not a defect — the scenario specifies it. Not random or approximate. *Collision:* testing vocabulary calls every deliberate variation a fault, which falsely changes its protocol standing. *Docs:* "the protocol-permitted perturbation." *Prompts:* "classify exact delay, valid notification traffic, correctness-preserving response reordering, or a stated permitted error response as a protocol-permitted perturbation, never an injected fault." *Violation:* "The out-of-order responses are an injected fault." → write: "The correctness-preserving response reordering is a protocol-permitted perturbation."

**T-injected-fault** — **injected fault.** The intentionally protocol-non-conforming behaviour a fault scenario produces at one stated method and invocation ordinal. Conforming instrument behaviour under that scenario (B-3). *Authority:* AR-3. *Related:* fault scenario, withhold, abort, malformed emission. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a protocol-permitted perturbation. Not an outcome or defect. Not chaos. *Collision:* the defect-handling reflex fires on this concept, while loose use also mislabels protocol-permitted response ordering and notification traffic as faults. *Docs:* "the injected fault." *Prompts:* "an injected fault intentionally violates or leaves unsatisfied a protocol obligation under a pinned fault scenario; it is conforming instrument behaviour and never a perturbation." *Violation:* "Fix the server so it stops dropping responses." → write: "The withholding fault scenario is pinned; the unsatisfied response obligation is the specified injected fault."

**T-injected-delay** — **injected delay.** A protocol-permitted perturbation that defers an emission by a stated amount before emitting it. *Authority:* AR-3. *Related:* perturbation scenario, withhold, subject timeout. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not an injected fault. Not withholding — a delayed response is eventually emitted; a withheld one never is. Not slowness or timeout. *Collision:* delay and withholding are habitually described with the same words, collapsing a permitted timing perturbation with an unsatisfied response obligation. *Docs:* "the injected delay." *Prompts:* "an injected delay defers and then emits; withholding never emits and is an injected fault." *Violation:* "The server is slow to respond, so the request times out." → write: "The injected delay defers the emission past the configured bound; the assertion suite adjudicates the subject timeout."

**T-subject-timeout** — **subject timeout.** The subject under test's act of ceasing to wait for a protocol response. Subject side. *Authority:* AR-7; holds no authority in this system. *Related:* injected delay, withhold, subject finding. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not the injected delay — the delay is instrument-side, the timeout is subject-side; write *injected delay*. Not withholding. Not an instrument behaviour of any kind. *Collision:* "it timed out" hides which side acted, and which side acted is the entire finding. *Docs:* "the subject timeout." *Prompts:* "timeouts are subject-side; the instrument withholds or delays and never times out." *Violation:* "The server timed out." → write: "The fixture server withheld the response; the subject under test reached its timeout."

**T-malformed-emission** — **malformed emission.** An injected fault in which the fixture server emits a message that is well-framed but carries a result the protocol does not permit. *Authority:* AR-3. *Related:* injected fault, protocol error response. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a protocol error response — an error response is permitted and well-formed; write *protocol error response*. Not corrupt framing — framing stays valid so the subject reaches its result-handling path; a distinct scenario is required for framing faults and none is currently designed (NC-3 does not cover this; flag the gap). Not a fixture defect. *Collision:* *malformed* invites readers to imagine garbage bytes; here the bytes are clean and the payload is deliberately wrong. *Docs:* "the malformed emission." *Prompts:* "a malformed emission is well-framed and carries an impermissible result; the framing is deliberately valid." *Violation:* "Send garbage to the client." → write: "Under the malformed-emission scenario the fixture server emits a well-framed message carrying an impermissible result."

**T-notification-burst** — **notification burst.** A protocol-permitted perturbation in which the fixture server emits a stated number of specified protocol notifications while an identified protocol request is outstanding. *Authority:* AR-3. *Related:* perturbation scenario, protocol notification. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not an injected fault merely because the notifications were unsolicited. Not noise, a load test, or concurrent traffic. Not a flood — the count and content are exact and bounded. *Collision:* *flood* and *unsolicited* imply abuse or protocol violation, although server notifications are ordinary protocol traffic. *Docs:* "the notification burst." *Prompts:* "the notification burst emits an exact, stated sequence of protocol notifications while one identified request is outstanding." *Violation:* "Spam the client with progress messages." → write: "Under the notification-burst perturbation scenario, the fixture server emits the stated notification sequence."

**T-response-reordering** — **response reordering.** A protocol-permitted perturbation in which the fixture server emits responses for outstanding protocol requests in a stated order other than arrival order, only where the reordering does not affect response correctness. *Authority:* AR-3. *Related:* perturbation scenario, protocol response, protocol request. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not LSP `textDocument/completion`, concurrency, or a race. Not always permitted — dependent operations must not be reordered when correctness would change. *Collision:* the former term *out-of-order completion* collided with the LSP completion feature and falsely labelled permitted response ordering as a fault. *Docs:* "response reordering." *Prompts:* "the pinned perturbation scenario states the exact response order and applies only where LSP permits reordering without affecting correctness." *Violation:* "Two requests race and finish in a random order." → write: "Under the response-reordering perturbation scenario, the fixture server emits the specified independent response second before the first."

**T-error-response-substitution** — **error-response substitution.** A protocol-permitted perturbation in which the fixture server emits a stated protocol error response — whose code and payload are permitted for the identified protocol request — in place of the result the corpus baseline fixes. The request's response obligation is satisfied. *Authority:* AR-3 produces it under the scenario pinned by AR-4. *Related:* perturbation scenario, protocol error response, protocol request. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not an injected fault — the emitted message is protocol-conforming and the response obligation is satisfied. Not a malformed emission — code and payload are permitted. Not withholding — a response is emitted. Not a fixture defect — the pinned scenario specifies it. *Collision:* the word *error* fires defect reflexes (Q-16, CM-3), inviting readers to classify a permitted error response as a fault or defect. *Docs:* "the error-response substitution." *Prompts:* "an error-response substitution emits a stated, permitted protocol error response in place of the baseline result; it is a protocol-permitted perturbation, never an injected fault." *Violation:* "Inject an error fault on workspace/symbol." → write: "Pin the error-response-substitution perturbation scenario stating the permitted error code and payload for the workspace/symbol request."

**T-invocation-ordinal** — **invocation ordinal.** The 1-based ordinal of a protocol request's arrival for a named method within one server lifetime. Scenarios trigger at a stated method and invocation ordinal. *Authority:* the pinned scenario states it (AR-4 pins); AR-3 matches arrivals against it. *Related:* scenario, protocol request, perturbation scenario, fault scenario, server lifetime. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not an occurrence — occurrences are appearances of identifiers in fixture-corpus source text; write *defining occurrence* or *referencing occurrence*. Not the JSON-RPC request identifier — identifiers are opaque values assigned by the sender. Not a wire position. *Collision:* *occurrence* previously carried both senses, tying scenario triggers to source text; request identifiers also read as ordinals but are opaque. *Docs:* "at invocation ordinal 2." *Prompts:* "a scenario triggers at a named method and invocation ordinal, counted per server lifetime; occurrences are source-text appearances only." *Violation:* "The scenario fires at occurrence 2 of references." → write: "The scenario triggers at invocation ordinal 2 of the references request."

### 7F — Outcomes, standing, and assurance

**T-conforming-outcome** — **conforming outcome.** An adjudicated outcome in which observed behaviour on the declared side matches the corpus baseline and pinned scenario: fixture-server behaviour in a self-check, or subject-under-test behaviour in a subject adjudication. *Authority:* AR-5. *Related:* adjudicate, non-conforming outcome, self-check. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a pass, green status, or proof beyond the declared side and scope. *Collision:* runner vocabulary and omitted side markers fuse instrument assurance with subject measurement. *Docs:* "a conforming self-check outcome" or "a conforming subject outcome." *Prompts:* "state the side: self-check outcome or subject outcome; the assertion suite adjudicates it conforming or non-conforming." *Violation:* "All tests passed, so normalization is correct." → write: "The declared subject outcomes were conforming for the corpus baseline and scenarios exercised."

**T-non-conforming-outcome** — **non-conforming outcome.** An adjudicated outcome in which observed behaviour on the declared side diverges from the corpus baseline or pinned scenario. A non-conforming self-check outcome is dispositioned as an instrument-side defect and never becomes a subject finding. A non-conforming subject outcome is dispositioned through FO-6 before attribution. *Authority:* AR-5. *Related:* FO-6, subject finding, fixture defect, self-check. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a subject finding until the FO-6 exclusion is complete. Not a failure or bug attribution. *Collision:* omitted side markers let an instrument defect become a Serena claim. *Docs:* "a non-conforming self-check outcome" or "a non-conforming subject outcome." *Prompts:* "declare the side; self-check outcomes stay instrument-side, and subject outcomes require the FO-6 exclusion before attribution." *Violation:* "Serena has a bug in cross-file references." → write: "A non-conforming subject outcome was adjudicated for cross-file referencing occurrences; apply FO-6 before recording any subject finding."

**T-subject-finding** — **subject finding.** A non-conforming subject outcome attributed to the subject under test after the FO-6 exclusion is complete and recorded. *Authority:* AR-5. *Related:* FO-5, FO-6, non-conforming outcome. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a baseline, fixture, launch-harness, or assertion-suite defect. Not a self-check outcome. Not an unexcluded divergence. *Collision:* readers treat "the test failed against Serena" as already being a finding against Serena. *Docs:* "the subject finding." *Prompts:* "a subject finding requires recorded exclusion of baseline, fixture, launch-harness, and assertion-suite defects." *Violation:* "This is a SolidLSP defect." → write: "After the recorded FO-6 exclusion, this is a subject finding against SolidLSP."

**T-fixture-defect** — **fixture defect.** Behaviour of the fixture server that conforms neither to the corpus baseline nor to the pinned scenario. *Authority:* AR-5 adjudicates; the instrument is fixed. *Related:* FO-2, injected fault, self-check. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not an injected fault — write *injected fault* for scenario-specified behaviour. Not a subject finding. *Collision:* every injected fault looks like a fixture defect to a reader without the scenario in view, and every fixture defect looks like a subject finding to a reader who trusts the instrument. *Docs:* "a fixture defect." *Prompts:* "check the pinned scenario before calling anything a fixture defect; specified behaviour is never a defect." *Violation:* "The server broke on the second references request." → write: "Either the pinned scenario specifies this at invocation ordinal 2, or it is a fixture defect; check the scenario before attributing."

**T-baseline-defect** — **baseline defect.** A disagreement between the expectation record and the fixture corpus text. *Authority:* AR-5 adjudicates; AR-1 ratifies the amendment. *Related:* FO-1, corpus baseline, ratify. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a fixture defect — the instrument may be right and the standard wrong. Not fixable by regenerating the expectation record from observed output — see T-expectation-record. *Collision:* the reflex on a mismatch is to trust the running code over the checked-in expectation, which is exactly backwards here. *Docs:* "a baseline defect." *Prompts:* "on a mismatch, the corpus baseline is not presumed wrong; a baseline defect is adjudicated and amended by hand, then re-ratified." *Violation:* "The expectations are stale; update them from the run." → write: "Adjudicate whether this is a baseline defect; if so, amend the expectation record by hand against the corpus text and re-ratify."

**T-launch-harness-defect** — **launch-harness defect.** Behaviour of the launch harness that violates its assembly, pinning, lifecycle, or resource-collection obligations. *Authority:* AR-5 adjudicates. *Related:* FO-3, launch harness. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not an assertion-suite defect, fixture defect, or subject finding. Not test flakiness — see F-12. *Collision:* the prior umbrella *harness defect* fused AR-4 assembly with AR-5 decision. *Docs:* "a launch-harness defect." *Prompts:* "scenario leakage, reused server lifetimes, incorrect external termination, and unreaped child processes are launch-harness defects." *Violation:* "The test is flaky." → write: "The reused server lifetime leaked pinned-scenario state; this is a launch-harness defect."

**T-assertion-suite-defect** — **assertion-suite defect.** Behaviour of the assertion suite that observes, compares, excludes, or adjudicates incorrectly. *Authority:* AR-5 adjudicates from independently produced evidence; AR-5's own unsupported assertion cannot exclude FO-4. *Related:* FO-4, assertion suite. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a launch-harness defect, fixture defect, or subject finding. *Collision:* calling every test-infrastructure problem a harness defect erases the decide/assemble split, while self-attestation would make the exclusion vacuous. *Docs:* "an assertion-suite defect." *Prompts:* "wrong comparison, wrong side attribution, or an invalid FO-6 exclusion is an assertion-suite defect; exclusion requires evidence independent of the behavior being checked." *Violation:* "The harness blamed Serena without exclusions." → write: "The assertion suite produced an invalid attribution; this is an assertion-suite defect."

**T-determinism-guarantee** — **determinism guarantee.** The ratified property that identical pinned scenario, identical corpus baseline, and identical protocol input sequence produce identical emissions, in identical order, on every supported operating system, without network access. *Authority:* AR-1 ratifies the guarantee in the contract set; AR-2 supplies the corpus baseline and AR-3 implements the run-time behaviour without thereby becoming co-authorities. *Related:* reproduction, scenario, injected fault. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not an aspiration or a joint authority invented from implementation roles. Not a claim about timing values — quantitative bounds are deferred (NC-4). Not a claim about the subject under test. *Collision:* fulfilment of a guarantee is easily mistaken for authority to establish it. *Docs:* "under the ratified determinism guarantee." *Prompts:* "cite the guarantee's ratified scope; implementation responsibility does not confer authority over the guarantee." *Violation:* "The fixture is deterministic." → write: "Under the ratified determinism guarantee, identical scenario, corpus baseline, and protocol input sequence produce identical emissions in identical order."

**T-reproduction** — **reproduction.** Re-running a recorded scenario, corpus baseline, and protocol input sequence and obtaining identical emissions. *Authority:* AR-4 performs it. *Related:* determinism guarantee, transcript. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a retry — a retry hopes for a different outcome; a reproduction requires the same one. Not a re-run of the assertion suite alone. *Collision:* *reproduce* in bug work means "get it to happen again, eventually"; here it means "get it to happen again, always." *Docs:* "the reproduction." *Prompts:* "a reproduction obtains identical emissions; anything intermittent is a defect, not a reproduction." *Violation:* "We reproduced it after a few tries." → write: "Intermittent recurrence is not a reproduction; adjudicate the source of nondeterminism as a defect."

**T-assurance-tier** — **assurance tier.** One of the named, closed groups into which adjudications are organised by what they measure and what they cost. *Authority:* AR-5. *Related:* self-check, fault scenario. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a priority. Not a severity. Not a directory layout. *Collision:* *tier* is used for importance elsewhere; here it is a measurement-class grouping only. *Docs:* "the assurance tier." *Prompts:* "assurance tiers group adjudications by what they measure; they carry no priority meaning." *Violation:* "Tier 1 issues are the most urgent." → write: "Assurance tiers group adjudications by measurement class; urgency is not a tier property."

**T-self-check** — **self-check.** Instrument assurance in which the assertion suite adjudicates fixture-server behaviour against the corpus baseline and pinned scenario. Instrument side. *Authority:* AR-5. *Related:* fixture defect, assurance tier, B-1. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a subject adjudication and not an exception that blurs B-1. It produces a conforming or non-conforming self-check outcome and never a subject finding. Not optional — an unassured instrument measures nothing. *Collision:* self-checks and subject adjudications may live in the same test tree, which lets instrument defects become Serena claims. *Docs:* "the self-check." *Prompts:* "self-checks assure the instrument; subject adjudications measure the subject under test; never combine their outcomes." *Violation:* "Our tests confirm the whole integration works." → write: "The self-checks assure the fixture server; separate subject adjudications measure Serena and SolidLSP."

**T-suite-assurance-adjudication** — **suite-assurance adjudication.** Instrument assurance in which the assertion suite adjudicates its own comparison behaviour against ratified known-outcome cases — cases whose conforming or non-conforming result the corpus baseline fixes in advance, including at least one deliberately non-conforming instrument case. Instrument side. *Authority:* AR-5 performs it; AR-2 fixes the known outcomes; AR-1 ratifies them. *Related:* self-check, assertion-suite defect, FO-6, assurance tier. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not a self-check — a self-check adjudicates fixture-server behaviour; this adjudicates the assertion suite's comparison behaviour. Not self-attestation — the known outcomes are fixed by the ratified corpus baseline, never by the assertion suite. Not proof of suite correctness beyond the exercised comparison class. *Collision:* an assertion suite that vouches for itself makes the FO-4 exclusion vacuous; the known-outcome construction moves the ground truth to AR-2. *Docs:* "the suite-assurance adjudication." *Prompts:* "an FO-4 exclusion cites a conforming suite-assurance adjudication of the same comparison class; the assertion suite's bare say-so excludes nothing." *Violation:* "The suite is trusted, so FO-4 is excluded." → write: "The conforming suite-assurance adjudication for this comparison class is cited; FO-4 is excluded on that evidence."

**T-support-registration** — **support registration.** The act of listing a language among Serena's production-supported languages. **Forbidden for the fixture language** (B-7). Named here so the prohibition is writable. *Authority:* outside this system entirely. *Related:* B-7, fixture language. *Aliases:* none. *Not:* not test-only wiring — a test-only project configuration routing fixture-language documents to the fixture server is permitted and is not a support registration; write *test-only routing*. *Collision:* the natural way to make a language work in Serena is to register it, and an agent following the ordinary path will cross B-7 without noticing. *Docs:* "support registration is forbidden for the fixture language." *Prompts:* "never add the fixture language to Serena's supported-language registry, documentation, or production configuration; test-only routing only." *Violation:* "Add the fixture language to the supported languages list." → write: "Add test-only routing for fixture-language documents; support registration is forbidden."

## 11. Term rankings

Semantic neighbourhoods. Every plausible near-synonym carries an explicit verdict, so that "an unlisted synonym is a violation" is enforceable. Notes state the behavioural reason only.

**R-1 — The instrument.**
The system's proper name is *LanguageServerFixture*; the process inside it is the *fixture server*. Never substitute one for the other.
Preferred: *fixture server*. Acceptable: *fixture LSP server* (documentation, after first use — the abbreviation adds nothing in prompts). Risky: *reference server* (permitted only where the sentence also names the subject under test; alone it reads as a specification-conformance reference implementation). Reject: *mock server*, *stub server*, *fake server*, *dummy server*, *test double*, *simulator*, *emulator*, *shim*, *toy server* — every one of these licenses hard-coded responses keyed by filename, which defeats document synchronization and symbolic-edit measurement.
Rejected alternative: *calibration server*. Calibration is an operation relating an instrument's indications to a reference standard; it does not accurately name this language-server process or the instrument/subject relation.

**R-2 — The expected model.**
Preferred: *expectation record*. Acceptable: *expected symbol graph* (documentation, first use only). Risky: none. Reject: *oracle* (dominant prior is a database vendor), *golden file*, *snapshot*, *approved output*, *fixture data*, *expected.json* used as a concept name rather than a filename — the snapshot family all license regeneration from observed output, which makes every adjudication vacuous.

**R-3 — No response arrives.**
Preferred: *withhold* / *withholding*. Acceptable: *withheld response*. Risky: none. Reject: *hang*, *freeze*, *wedge*, *stall*, *block*, *deadlock*, *drop*, *swallow* — all name defects, and an agent that reads a defect will repair it, deleting the scenario.

**R-4 — Ending a server lifetime.**
Preferred: *retire* (protocol-driven, by the fixture server), *abort* (fault-scenario-driven, by the fixture server), *terminate externally* (by the launch harness), *reap* (post-exit collection by the launch harness). Acceptable: none. Risky: *exit* (permitted only as the protocol notification or observed process state, never as an authority-restricted act). Reject: *kill*, *terminate* bare, *stop*, *close*, *shut down* as an act, *teardown*, *cleanup*, *dispose* — each hides actor, origin, or the distinction between termination and collection.

**R-5 — The measured party.**
Preferred: *subject under test*. Acceptable: *SUT* (documentation, after first use); *Serena* or *SolidLSP* where the claim is layer-specific and the layer is the point. Risky: *the integration* (permitted only where both layers are meant and named). Reject: *client*, *consumer*, *caller*, *the user*, *the host* — each inverts or obscures the measurement relation.

**R-6 — A named construct in source.**
Preferred: *fixture declaration*; *corpus declaration path* for its identity. Acceptable: *declaration* bare inside a paragraph that already wrote *fixture declaration*. Risky: *in-file declaration path* (permitted only when the fixture-corpus-relative source path is also present). Reject: *symbol*, *name*, *entity*, *node*, *item* — *symbol* spans four layers and bare *name* or in-file path alone is not corpus-unique.

**R-7 — Appearances in source.**
Preferred: *defining occurrence*, *referencing occurrence*. Acceptable: *occurrence* where the kind is stated in the same sentence. Risky: none. Reject: *reference*, *usage*, *mention*, *hit*, *match* — all fuse the source-text appearance with the protocol request and with the subject-side concept.

**R-8 — Positions.**
Preferred: *wire position*. Acceptable: *byte offset*, *scalar offset*, *grapheme offset* — each permitted only with its unit named and marked non-wire. Risky: none. Reject: *position* bare, *offset* bare, *index*, *column*, *char*, *character position* — the error is invisible on ASCII input and appears only where the corpus's non-ASCII identifiers are, which is where it matters.

**R-9 — Non-baseline instrument behaviour.**
Preferred: *protocol-permitted perturbation* under a perturbation scenario; *injected fault* under a fault scenario. Acceptable: *the pinned scenario's behaviour* after its class is named. Risky: none. Reject: *failure*, *error*, *fault* bare, *misbehaviour*, *bad behaviour*, *chaos*, *fuzzing* — these either route the reader to defect handling or erase protocol-conformance status.

**R-10 — Adjudicated standing.**
Preferred: *conforming self-check outcome*, *non-conforming self-check outcome*, *conforming subject outcome*, *non-conforming subject outcome*, *subject finding*. Acceptable: none. Risky: none. Reject: *pass*, *fail*, *green*, *red*, *success*, *failure*, *bug*, *regression* — runner and issue-tracker vocabulary omits the side and skips the FO-6 exclusion.

**R-11 — The whole artefact's relation to Serena.**
Preferred: *test-only routing*. Acceptable: *test-only project configuration*. Risky: none. Reject: *language support*, *supported language*, *language integration*, *register the language*, *production wiring* — following the ordinary Serena path crosses B-7 without the agent noticing.

## 12. Behaviour triggers

Term families that switch the reader into a required checking behaviour. These fire at the moment of writing.

**BT-1 — Any authority-restricted verb** (ratify, pin, launch, emit, withhold, abort, retire, terminate externally, reap, record, adjudicate, enforce) → verify the grammatical subject is that act's authority in §1. If it is not, rewrite with propose, submit, request, claim, or declare, and name the real authority.

**BT-2 — Any of: expected, expectation, baseline, correct, should be** → check direction. Does anything flow toward the fixture server? If yes, it is a B-2 violation; rewrite so the expectation reaches the assertion suite only.

**BT-3 — Any of: symbol, reference, definition, declaration path, range, position, offset** → check that the representation layer (B-6), corpus declaration path components, and position unit (B-4) required by the sentence are named. Add every missing qualifier before continuing.

**BT-4 — Any of: fail, failure, error, crash, hang, broken, timeout, unhealthy, fault** → first classify the pinned scenario. Protocol-permitted variation is a perturbation; intentional protocol non-conformance is an injected fault; unspecified behaviour is a defect dispositioned through FO-1 to FO-5. Name the class and act before attributing a party.

**BT-5 — Any of: shutdown, exit, stop, kill, terminate, reap, cleanup, teardown** → check B-5. Name whether the fixture server retired or aborted, or the launch harness terminated externally. Then state whether the launch harness reaped the already-exited child.

**BT-6 — Any of: support, supported, language, register, production, integration** → check B-7. If the sentence puts the fixture language into Serena's supported set, it is a violation; rewrite as test-only routing.

**BT-7 — Any mechanism noun** (index, parser, cache, transcript, hash, proof, validation) → attach the non-authority marker before reasoning further, and confirm the sentence does not make validity depend on the mechanism's availability.

**BT-8 — Any transformation word applied to a subject-side artefact** (normalized, parsed, resolved, converted, cleaned) → append the standing rider "standing unchanged" unless an explicit act from §2 is present in the sentence.

**BT-9 — Any statement sourced from the subject under test or from a tool result** → classify it as an untrusted claim, not as a fact about the corpus. The corpus baseline states what is true of the corpus.

**BT-10 — Any of: random, retry, eventually, sometimes, usually, occasionally, race** → stop. This system has no such behaviour. Either the scenario states method and invocation ordinal exactly, or a defect is being described as a property.

**BT-11 — Any word from the engine's process vocabulary** (arm, loop, conductor, trio, register, unit, lane, and the other tokens the engine reserves) → check whether the engine's process is actually meant. If this system is meant, none of these words applies; use §2's acts.

## 13. Pasteable rules

Each rule is self-contained and binds alone. Ordered by catastrophe.

- **RULE-1** The fixture server is the measuring instrument; Serena and SolidLSP are the subject under test. Self-checks assure the instrument; separate subject adjudications measure the subject. A self-check never produces a subject finding.
- **RULE-2** The expectation record is authored by hand and read only by the assertion suite. The fixture server must not be able to reach it, import it, or share a module with it.
- **RULE-3** Never regenerate an expectation from observed output. Amend it by hand against the corpus text and re-ratify the corpus baseline.
- **RULE-4** Behaviour specified by a pinned scenario is conforming instrument behaviour. Classify protocol-permitted variation as a perturbation and intentional protocol non-conformance as an injected fault; never repair either as an unspecified defect.
- **RULE-5** LanguageServerFixture negotiates or falls back to UTF-16 as a ratified instrument invariant; its wire positions are zero-based line and zero-based UTF-16 code-unit offsets. LSP itself also permits UTF-8 and UTF-32.
- **RULE-6** Name the layer in every sentence about symbols: fixture declaration, symbol emission, normalized symbol, or tool result. Never one word for two layers.
- **RULE-7** Match declarations by corpus declaration path: fixture-corpus-relative source path plus in-file declaration path. Never use a bare name or in-file path alone.
- **RULE-8** A protocol request carries an identifier and is owed exactly one response at the protocol layer; a notification carries none and is owed nothing. Only a pinned fault scenario may intentionally leave a request obligation unsatisfied.
- **RULE-9** Retire, abort, external termination, and reap are four distinct acts. Reaping collects an already-exited child and never ends a running server lifetime.
- **RULE-10** Every non-baseline scenario adjudication gets its own server lifetime. A scenario is pinned once per server lifetime and never changes within it.
- **RULE-11** The launch harness assembles, the assertion suite adjudicates, the CI gate enforces. Never collapse two of the three.
- **RULE-12** A non-conforming subject outcome is a baseline, fixture, launch-harness, or assertion-suite defect until all four are excluded and recorded. Only then is it a subject finding.
- **RULE-13** The fixture language is never registered as a Serena supported language. Test-only routing only.
- **RULE-14** Never write *fixture*, *server*, *client*, *symbol*, *reference*, *definition*, *declaration path*, *position*, *range*, *version*, *test*, *lifetime*, *Pin*, or *closed* bare. Use the qualified form from §9.
- **RULE-15** Never write *deterministic* as a bare adjective. Cite the determinism guarantee and its scope.
- **RULE-16** Only ratify and adjudicate confer standing. Other ACT-n entries change run-time state, produce evidence, collect resources, or execute a consequence. No participle — validated, verified, checked, approved — changes standing.
- **RULE-17** Nothing in this system is random, flaky, or eventual. Every scenario states its method and its occurrence exactly.
- **RULE-18** When work requires naming something this lexicon does not name, do not coin and do not borrow. Flag the naming gap to the accountable authority and proceed without naming it.
- **RULE-19** LanguageServerFixture is the system's proper name, written in full and never abbreviated or contracted. `fixture_langserv` names only the repository and the installation directory, never the system in prose. The instrument is never written as belonging to, shipped by, or endorsed by any subject it measures.

## 15. Enforcement

**Three finding tiers.**
- **BANNED** — hard fail; fixed before delivery. Anything in §10; an authority-restricted verb with a non-authority subject; a banned compound.
- **REVIEW** — flagged for judgment; the reviewer decides and records the decision. Risky-ranked terms in §11; patterns near reserved verbs; unnamed layers.
- **QUALIFY** — a bare overloaded noun from §9; the qualified form is required.

**Mechanizable:** forbidden-term matching (§10); bare-noun detection (§9); subject-lock checks at the pattern level — a §2 verb with a non-§1 subject nearby; unlisted-alias detection against §7 alias lists; banned-compound detection; bare *deterministic*; positions written without a unit.

**Needs judgment:** whether a Risky term's licensing condition is met; register fit; whether a REVIEW finding is a true violation; whether an FO-6 exclusion is genuine.

**Exemptions.** The conformance checker never runs on this document, nor on any artifact that quotes banned forms in order to ban them. Exempt: this lexicon; any method document. List additions here by amendment.

**Vocabulary conformance is a gate, not a style guide.** Run the checker on every artifact before delivery. Fix BANNED. Adjudicate REVIEW and QUALIFY and record the adjudications in the artifact's change log. An artifact is not ratifiable with open BANNED findings. Vocabulary conformance says nothing by itself about a self-check outcome or subject outcome.
